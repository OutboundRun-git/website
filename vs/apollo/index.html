OutboundRun vs Apollo.

Apollo is a contact database and sequencer built for SDR-driven volume outbound. OutboundRun is a per-account research and per-persona drafting layer built for enterprise AEs. Same channel, different jobs. Here is how they compare, honestly.

Honest strengths of each.

Where OutboundRun wins
  • Depth per account. Every touch is grounded in specific, cited facts pulled from SEC filings, earnings calls, and live news. Apollo emails read the same for 500 accounts.
  • Per-persona drafting. A CDO gets a different email than the CFO at the same company. Apollo relies on template variables, which stay generic.
  • Enterprise-friendly. Local-first architecture means no data-residency conversations with your security team. Apollo is cloud SaaS.
  • Purpose-built for AEs. The workflow assumes low-volume, high-depth touches. Apollo is optimized for SDR throughput.
Where Apollo wins
  • Contact discovery at scale. Apollo's database is one of the largest B2B contact sources on the market. OutboundRun expects you to bring your accounts and use your own contact-discovery tool.
  • Multi-touch sequences. If you need to send 8 emails across 4 weeks with A/B testing, Apollo does that. OutboundRun drafts one high-quality first touch and hands off.
  • Volume workflows. If your motion is SDRs sending hundreds of touches a day, Apollo is built for that shape. OutboundRun is not.
When to pick which

The short answer.

Pick Apollo if your motion is SDR-led, high-volume, and you need contact data + sequencing in one tool. Apollo is the default for a reason.

Pick OutboundRun if you are an enterprise AE running an ABM motion on a defined patch, and the first touch on each account has to actually land. You would rather send 20 great emails than 500 generic ones.

They coexist. Some teams use Apollo for the top of the funnel and OutboundRun for the strategic accounts where a template will not cut it. That is a valid stack.
